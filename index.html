About the Guild

LootTheBox is a retail and entertainment corporation that sells mystery boxes blending physical collectibles, such as scannable keychains and pins, with digital in-game rewards. Owned by shareholders and overseen by a board of directors, the company utilizes a structured leadership team to ensure strategic planning and responsible decision-making. This corporate framework promotes operational efficiency and scalability, allowing the business to raise capital and maintain long-term continuity beyond individual ownership.

Library of Scrolls

Records, development logs, and guild discoveries from the LootTheBox archives.

1. The Formation of LootTheBox

Posted by: Giosef Yatar | February 6, 2026

LootTheBox originally started as a game development group formed to create interactive digital experiences. During the early planning stage, the team focused on organizing responsibilities, discussing game concepts, and building a structured development workflow. Each member was assigned a role based on their strengths, including programming, operations, design, finance, and marketing. As development continued, the team later expanded the company’s vision by introducing physical mystery box products connected to digital rewards and collectibles.

2. Game Planning, Story Development, and First Systems

Posted by: Justine Lloyd De Castro | March 11, 2026

After establishing LootTheBox as a game development company, the team moved forward with planning its first official project: a PC Building Simulator RPG. The goal was to create a system where players progress through building and upgrading computer systems while also experiencing RPG-style mechanics such as quests, resource management, and progression-based rewards.

The planning phase focused on defining the core gameplay loop—acquiring parts, assembling PCs, optimizing builds, and completing objectives tied to performance and efficiency. This structure was designed to blend technical simulation with RPG progression systems.

Alongside gameplay design, the team developed the early story concept. The narrative revolves around a world where technology progression is tied to building powerful systems, and players advance by completing tasks that require strategic PC assembly and upgrades.

Initial system development began shortly after planning. This included early frameworks for inventory management, part categorization, stat-based components, and basic upgrade mechanics. These systems served as the foundation for how players interact with hardware and progression within the game.

This stage marked the transition from conceptual planning into structured game development for a PC Building Simulator RPG.

3. Business Planning and Company Strategy Development

Posted by: Rhianne Cate Capistrano | March 23, 2026

Following the establishment of the PC Building Simulator RPG project, LootTheBox transitioned into defining its business structure and long-term strategy. The focus shifted from purely development work to planning how the company would operate, grow, and sustain its projects.

The business plan outlined the company’s core objectives, including developing interactive games, integrating physical and digital reward systems, and building a scalable model for future products. This phase also involved identifying target audiences, potential revenue streams, and the overall direction of the brand.

A key part of the planning process was defining operational roles within the organization. Responsibilities were organized across development, operations, finance, marketing, and support to ensure efficient workflow and accountability within the team.

In addition, the company began structuring its long-term vision, which includes expanding beyond game development into a hybrid entertainment system combining gameplay experiences with collectible-based rewards.

This stage marked the formal transition of LootTheBox from a development-focused team into a structured game company with a defined business strategy.

4. Game Development: Building the PC Building Simulator RPG

Posted by: Justine Lloyd De Castro | May 4, 2026

After finalizing the business strategy, LootTheBox moved into full game development for its PC Building Simulator RPG. This phase focused on turning planned systems and concepts into an actual playable experience.

The development process began with building the core gameplay loop, where players acquire computer parts, assemble systems, and evaluate performance based on in-game objectives. Each component was designed with specific stats and attributes to simulate realistic PC building mechanics while still supporting RPG-style progression.

The team also implemented the early version of the inventory system, allowing players to store, manage, and select hardware components. This system became essential for structuring gameplay flow and ensuring that upgrades and item management were intuitive and functional.

Parallel to system development, the game’s interface began taking shape. Early UI elements were created to support navigation, part selection, and system feedback during PC assembly. These elements were continuously adjusted to improve clarity and usability.

This stage represents the shift from planning and documentation into active construction of the PC Building Simulator RPG, where core mechanics began forming into a playable structure.

5. After Hours: Final Game Identity and Alpha Phase

Posted by: Giosef Yatar | May 20, 2026

As development progressed, the project officially adopted its final identity under the name After Hours. The title reflects the game’s theme of late-stage progression, technical problem-solving, and building systems beyond standard limits, aligning with the PC Building Simulator RPG concept.

With the game identity finalized, the project entered its alpha phase. This stage focused on stabilizing core mechanics and testing the overall gameplay loop in a functional environment. Systems such as PC assembly, component management, and performance evaluation were reviewed for consistency and balance.

During alpha development, the primary goal was to identify issues in gameplay flow and refine interactions between systems. Early builds were tested internally to ensure that core mechanics were working as intended before further expansion.

This phase also marked the beginning of structured iteration, where feedback from testing was used to improve stability, adjust mechanics, and prepare the game for future development stages beyond alpha.

The transition into After Hours alpha represents a major milestone in the project, where the game shifted from full development into structured testing and refinement.
